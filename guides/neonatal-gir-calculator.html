Neonatal GIR Calculator: Formula and How to Calculate GIR

A practical guide to the neonatal glucose infusion rate (GIR) formula, multiple glucose sources and use of the free neonatal GIR calculator.

What is GIR?

Glucose infusion rate (GIR) expresses the amount of glucose delivered per kilogram of body weight per minute, in mg/kg/min. It can be calculated from the glucose concentration and infusion rate of a glucose-containing fluid.

GIR (mg/kg/min) = dextrose concentration (%) × infusion rate (mL/kg/day) ÷ 144

For a fluid volume entered as mL/day:

GIR = concentration (%) × volume (mL/day) × 10 ÷ [weight (kg) × 1440]

The two expressions are mathematically equivalent. The calculator uses the second form internally when fluid volume is entered in mL/day.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the infant's weight. The calculator accepts kg, g or lb.
  2. Enter the planned total fluid intake (TFI) in mL/kg/day.
  3. Enter a target GIR when a target is being used clinically.
  4. Add only the IV or oral fluids actually being administered.
  5. Review current GIR, remaining TFI and nutritional contributions.
  6. If a target GIR is entered, review the interactive TFI-aware plan and verify the proposed fluid concentrations and volumes before use.

Multiple fluid sources

When more than one glucose-containing source is administered, their GIR contributions are additive. The calculator therefore sums the contribution from each selected fluid. Fluids without glucose contribution, such as saline, do not add to GIR but still contribute to TFI.

For milk or a custom fluid, the entered carbohydrate/glucose composition is used for the calculator's GIR calculation. This is a calculation convention and should be interpreted in the clinical context of the infant's actual nutrition and glucose management.

Example

For a 1 kg infant receiving 100 mL/day of D10:

GIR = 10 × 100 × 10 ÷ (1 × 1440) = 6.94 mg/kg/min

Always verify the actual concentration, infusion rate and patient weight before relying on a calculated value.

Clinical safety

This page explains the calculation method; it does not provide a treatment target for an individual infant. Blood glucose, clinical condition, fluid status, route, vascular access, product concentration and local neonatal protocols must be considered separately.
